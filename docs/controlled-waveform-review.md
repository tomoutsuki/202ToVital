# Controlled waveform comparison (2026-10-09)

Data: `../.materials/Controlled/single_note_waveshape/`. The five source presets differ in `O1TY` (0 saw, 1 pulse/square, 2 sine, 3 triangle, 4 Super Saw); OSC1 is selected by `BLNC=-1`, filters are bypassed (`F1TY=F2TY=0`), and effects selectors are off. The source `original_wav/*.wav` files contain **AAC audio in QuickTime MOV containers** despite their names. The converted recordings are 48 kHz stereo PCM WAV. The originals are 44.1 kHz stereo AAC. This comparison decodes each by container, measures 300 ms of steady sound starting 60 ms after the note onset, and compares harmonic amplitudes relative to the fundamental. Exact phase, transient details, and human perception are not represented by the harmonic score.

The original recordings were played at **MIDI note 72** and the supplied Vital recordings at **MIDI note 60**, confirmed by the user. Their fundamentals are approximately 523.27 Hz and 261.66 Hz, a 2:1 ratio. This is an input-note difference, **not evidence of a converter tuning error**. All supplied clips share approximately 2.18–2.73 s active note timing. For a pitch-matched check, Vita 0.1.0 loaded each supplied `.vital` and rendered MIDI note 72; those renders are in `output/matched_MIDI72/`, aligned to the original onset for direct audition. Vita was given a silent preroll to let control smoothing settle. These previews use Vita rather than the user's Waveform export path; the host/export chain creates an additional ~3.2 dB level difference.

The source/Vital level differences use steady RMS. The supplied Vital WAVs are about 3.2 dB quieter than a direct Vita render of their `.vital` files at the same MIDI note; host/export gain therefore also affects the supplied file comparison. The converter's default profile adds -18 dB of output headroom. Adjust playback level for listening tests without changing source audio. No loudness target can yet be inferred for all Poison patches.

At the time of this review, Super Saw loaded a saw wavetable with seven Vital unison voices and set unison blend and detune to zero at `O1PW=0` and `O1PM=0`. The supplied Vital Super Saw and saw WAVs were effectively identical (normalized sample correlation >0.9999999), while Poison's Super Saw and saw recordings were distinct (correlation ~0.161). A later, denser MIX/DETUNE sweep found a JP-8000/Szabo-style nonlinear control law and now drives a fitted Vital Blend ratio and outer-voice detune in the converter. The current converter additionally embeds the supplied `JP_inspired_HP2_fc_1p5xF0_2048` single-cycle table for every Super Saw oscillator. Its contribution to the remaining waveform, phase and asymmetric-voice-layout differences still needs independent validation.

**Verdict:** As supplied, the render pairs will not sound sufficiently similar because their input notes differ by an octave and their levels differ substantially. When the MIDI notes and listening levels match, saw, sine, square and triangle have very close steady harmonic structures. Super Saw still does not match well. This is an objective spectral assessment; a listening judgement and tests across more notes, velocities and Mix/Detune settings remain to be done.

Reproduce with `PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python tools/compare_controlled_waveforms.py ../.materials/Controlled/single_note_waveshape -o output/waveform-comparison.json --matched-midi 72 --write-matched-dir output/matched_MIDI72`. The JSON artifact records frequencies, levels and the first 24 relative harmonic amplitudes. The Python tool needs optional `av`, `numpy` and `vita` packages in the project virtual environment.
